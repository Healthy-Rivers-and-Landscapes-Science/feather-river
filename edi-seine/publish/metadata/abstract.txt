This dataset contains fish catch and habitat observations from beach seine surveys conducted by the California Department of Water Resources (DWR) on the lower Feather River, California, from 1997 to 2025. The surveys began in the late 1990s to document the species composition, distribution, relative abundance and migration timing of fishes in the river, and to supplement rotary screw trap monitoring, which operates at fixed locations and is less effective at capturing larger juvenile salmonids. Juvenile Chinook salmon (Oncorhynchus tshawytscha) and steelhead (O. mykiss) are the target species, but all species captured are recorded. The study area extends from the Fish Barrier Dam (river mile 67) to the confluence with the Sacramento River (river mile 0) and includes the low flow channel and the high flow channel; sites in this dataset span river miles 12.5 to 66.5. Fish were captured with beach seines, most often a 50-foot seine, using a sweeping technique in open habitat and a box or ramp technique at boat ramps and backwaters. Captured fish were identified to the lowest possible taxonomic level and counted. A subsample of each species (generally up to 50 salmonids and up to 20 other fish per haul) was measured for fork length. Salmonids were assigned a life stage and Chinook salmon a run. Each record also carries the sample date, location, river mile, gear, haul length, width and depth, water temperature, a rating of how the haul went, and habitat descriptors (substrate, cover and stream feature). The 60,699 records come from about 2,000 seine samples at 42 river-mile sites (225 recorded location names) and were compiled from three source periods (1997-2001, 2008-2014 and 2015-2025). Sampling occurred in 1997-2001, 2008-2009, 2013-2014 and 2015-2025, mostly between January and August; no data are included for 2002-2007 or 2010-2012. Sites were generally revisited monthly, but substitute sites were used when flow prevented sampling, the lowermost 20 river miles were under-sampled, and some samples targeted hatchery releases, so these data should not be treated as a random or complete census of the fish community. Records from 1997-2001 are individual fish, whereas 2008-2025 records carry a count. Haul dimensions and depths are in meters; the definition of seine area differs between source periods and is described in the attribute definitions. These data are published to support the Healthy Rivers and Landscapes Science Program.

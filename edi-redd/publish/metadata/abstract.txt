Since 2014, the California Department of Water Resources (DWR) has conducted annual Chinook salmon redd surveys on the lower Feather River. The objective of this data collection effort is to quantify and understand potential shifts in redd distribution and potential habitat differences between historic and restored sites. The redd surveys are conducted in the uppermost 16 miles of the lower Feather River, though they have been concentrated in the uppermost two miles in, and adjacent to, the Gravel Supplementation Areas (GSAs). Redd surveys are conducted less frequently in the 14 miles downstream of the GSAs. Surveys typically begin in mid-September at the onset of spawning and generally conclude at the end of November. Through 2023, redds were located and measured by ground-based visual surveys. Beginning in 2024, DWR began surveying redds primarily by unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV, or drone) flights along fixed flight routes, with orthomosaic imagery from each flight used to mark and count redds; ground surveys continued at a subset of sites as a supplement to the drone surveys, and some sites were surveyed using both methods. This dataset includes redd counts and locations, redd physical measurements (depth, velocity, substrate, and size, where collected), survey site dates and areas, and records of surveyed locations with zero redds. The 2024 and 2025 drone survey data and the UAV flight route schedule are new in this version, and 2024 and 2025 ground survey data have been appended. This dataset represents an extensive time series that could be used to identify habitat conditions where Chinook salmon spawn and how these conditions have changed over time, especially in areas where restoration has occurred. These data were published to support the Healthy Rivers and Landscapes Program.
